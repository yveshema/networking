Week 3 — instructor notes

Shape of the week

The heaviest week so far, and the first graded one. Milestone 1 is handed out at the end of session 1 so students have the whole week to see the activities as preparation.

Session Do Note
1 A1, A2 hand out milestone 1 in the last 10 minutes
2 A4, A3 A4 first — it is the week's second anchor and A3 is short
3 A5, then A7 if time A5 needs a full hour; A7 works well as a closer with no machines

Compressed: A1, A4, A5. A2 and A3 are each 25–35 minutes and can be homework.

RAM budget

Both role templates used this week are 512 MB — OL9 Switch and OL9 PC — so the whole week is cheaper than it looks. The built-in Ethernet switch in A3 costs nothing.

Activity Nodes RAM Notes
A1 1 Switch 512 MB + 4 VPCS (negligible)
A2 1 Switch + 1 PC 1 GB PC1 must be an OL9 PC
A3 4 PCs 2 GB all four must be OL9 PCs — VPCS silently under-counts
A4 1–2 Switches 0.5–1 GB 2 for the trunk section
A5 2 Switches 1 GB + 4 VPCS
A6 1 Docker + hosts — Docker on the server required
A8 2 Switches + 2 PCs 2 GB the week's longest activity

A3 and A8 tie at 2 GB, which is the week's peak and comfortable on any machine that can run the GNS3 VM at all. If you are working from older notes that put A3 at 4 GB, that was the general-purpose node at 1 GB each; the PC template halves it.

What actually goes wrong

  • br_netfilter. Bridged frames get handed to iptables and pings fail with a correct-looking bridge. sysctl -w net.bridge.bridge-nf-call-iptables=0. This will bite in A1, A4 and A5. Consider setting it in the image.
  • A1's ageing time is in centiseconds and iproute2 is otherwise consistent about seconds. Students set 3000 expecting 3000 seconds. Have them cross-check with cat /sys/class/net/br0/bridge/ageing_time.
  • A4's vid 1 deletion is the single most-skipped line in the week. Without it, VLANs appear not to work and students conclude they mistyped something. Put it on the board.
  • A2 requires PC1 to be OL9. Students who try ip neigh in VPCS get a syntax error and assume the activity is broken.
  • A5's storm can lock a slow laptop. Show them the toolbar stop button before they build it. Also insist they remove the second link promptly — students leave it running while discussing.
  • A5 part 3 convergence takes ~30 s and students conclude it failed at 10 s. Say the number in advance.

Things to say out loud

  • After A1's ageing demo: "That table is never right. It is only recently correct. Every switch you will ever touch is working from a slightly stale memory."
  • After A4: "You broke that network on purpose and it is now more correct than it was. Every campus network you have used is deliberately broken this way."
  • After A5 part 1: "Nothing was wrong with either switch. Both behaved exactly as designed. The fault was in the topology, and the topology was the thing you were told to add for reliability."
  • After A7 question 3: ask which cable is shared. Wait.

The platform gap, handled

CSMA/CD and CSMA/CA cannot be demonstrated in GNS3 at all. There is no shared medium and no collision model; the hub does not collide either. A7 exists because of that, and it teaches the topic better than an animation would — students who have had to stop mid-word remember why jam signals exist.

If you want the picture as well, Packet Tracer animates a collision in Simulation mode. Ten minutes, projected, is enough.

Assessment

Milestone 1 opens this week. See ../../project/milestone1-switching/. The activities are ungraded and are deliberately a superset of the techniques the milestone needs — A1 gives them the table, A4 the VLANs, A5 the redundancy, A2 the evidence technique for proving traffic is absent.

Watch for the common misread: students think the milestone is A1–A5 repeated with more nodes. It is not — it gives requirements and no commands, and the marks are in the design decisions and the evidence. Say that when handing it out.
