Week 5 — instructor notes

Shape of the week

Session Do Note
1 A1, A2 A2 is the week's anchor; give it the time
2 A3, A4 both are arithmetic checked by building
3 A5 parts 1–3 part 4 (peer review) as homework, due next week

Compressed: A2 and A5. A1 is a good ten-minute demonstration; A3 and A4 can be homework with the worksheet as the deliverable.

The pedagogical bet of this week

Subnetting arithmetic still has to be learned and practised on paper — nothing here replaces that. What changes is where the feedback comes from. A wrong boundary produces a host that cannot reach its gateway, in the same session, unambiguously. Students correct themselves before a mark is involved.

Keep worksheet problems as homework if you already have a bank of them. The activities are the check, not the substitute.

What actually goes wrong

  • A2 is invisible unless redirects are suppressed. sysctl -w net.ipv4.conf.all.send_redirects=0 on R1 is in the build for a reason: with redirects on, H2 learns a host route after the first ping and the asymmetry disappears mid-activity. Have them turn redirects back on afterwards and watch the ICMP redirect arrive — it is a good second half.
  • A2's addresses are load-bearing. .11 must fall outside the /25 and .200 inside it. Students who pick their own numbers often choose two that are both in the upper half, and nothing happens.
  • A3's /31 needs exactly two hosts on the segment. A third node on the switch will make it fail confusingly.
  • A3's "use the broadcast address anyway" has genuinely variable outcomes across stacks. The activity asks students to record what they see rather than confirm a prediction; mark it that way. A student who reports "it was accepted and nothing obvious broke" has observed correctly.
  • A4's overlap section: the point is that no error appears. Students will hunt for one. Tell them in advance that silence is the finding.
  • A5 part 1 must be finished before anyone opens GNS3. Students who build first and calculate afterwards get a working network by trial and error and learn nothing. Collect the plans, or at least sight them, before the build starts.

Things to say out loud

  • After A1: "The switch, the cable and the other host were never consulted. A subnet is a belief held by hosts, and the network has no opinion about it."
  • After A2: "This network passes every test you would run and is wrong. It will keep working until something unrelated fails, and then the obvious explanation will be the wrong one."
  • After A3's /31: "'Subtract two' has a reason. When you know the reason you can tell when it stops applying, and that is the difference between knowing a rule and understanding it."
  • Before A5 part 4: "Your plan is perfectly clear to you. That is not the test."

Assessment

  • A1–A4: worksheet, ungraded or lightly graded.
  • A5 is graded as a lab, with its own rubric in the activity file. Half the marks are on the plan and the evidence, not the build.
  • The running project is not re-addressed this week. Milestones 1 and 2 use addressing you supplied, deliberately: a bad subnet plan should cost a lab, not cascade into the project. If you later add a milestone 3 that re-addresses the project network from a student-chosen block, A5 is its rehearsal — see ../../project/README.md.

Prep checklist

  • [ ] Paper subnetting practice available for students who need more repetition
  • [ ] A2's exact addresses on the board (.11 with /24, .200 with /25)
  • [ ] A5 briefs printed; peer-review pairings decided in advance
  • [ ] Milestone 2 progress checked — week 5 is when a struggling student becomes visible
  • [ ] Decision recorded on whether a milestone 3 will re-address the project network
