Week 5 — Subnetting

Assumes: weeks 1–4. Students can build routers, read routing tables, and have already met a /30 transit link and a /25 that broke a working network in week 1.

Leaves students able to: state what a mask actually does to a host's behaviour; recognise a mask mismatch from its one-directional symptom; locate a subnet's boundaries by measurement rather than arithmetic alone; and produce a VLSM plan that survives being built by somebody else.

The arc

Subnetting is normally taught as arithmetic and assessed on paper, which is why students who can convert masks flawlessly still cannot fix a network. This week keeps the arithmetic — there is no substitute — but every answer is checked by building it, so a wrong bit-count shows up as a failed ping rather than a red mark.

The week is ordered by consequence, not by mechanics: what a mask does to one host → what happens when two hosts disagree about it → where the edges are → splitting a block → design something real.

# Activity Time Needs
A1 The mask is a claim 35 min 2 PCs, 1 switch
A2 The asymmetric mask ★ 45 min 1 router, 2 PCs, 1 switch
A3 Boundaries, measured 40 min 3 PCs, 1 switch
A4 Splitting a block 45 min 1 router, 4 hosts, 4 switches
A5 Design it, then build it ★ 90 min 1 router, 4 hosts, 4 switches

★ = keep if compressed.

Why A2 is the anchor

A mask mismatch is the failure that paper cannot teach. Two hosts on one wire, one with /24 and one with /25, produce a network where traffic flows one way directly and the other way via a router — which works, or half-works, or works until the router is removed, and looks like nothing else. Students who have seen it once recognise it for the rest of their careers.

Deliverable

worksheet.md, plus A5's design document, which is graded as a lab (not part of the running project — see below).

Relationship to the running project

Milestones 1 and 2 are already built and graded, on addressing you handed out. A5 is where students choose addressing for the first time, on a separate topology, so that a bad plan costs a lab rather than the project.

A future milestone 3 would re-address the project network from a block the students subnet themselves — which is exactly what A5 rehearses. See ../../project/README.md.
