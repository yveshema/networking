Week 2 — instructor notes

Shape of the week

Session Do Note
1 A1, A3 A1 is the week's anchor; A3 is short and fills the rest
2 A2 needs the full session; it is the hardest and most valuable
3 A4, then A5 or A6 A5 sets up week 3; A6 closes the packet-vs-circuit topic

Compressed to one session: A1 then A2, nothing else.

RAM budget

This is the first week that can exhaust a student laptop. The OL9 template asks for 1 GB per node.

Activity OL9 nodes Approx. RAM
A1 0 negligible
A2 1 1 GB
A3 2 2 GB
A4 3 3 GB
A5 0 negligible
A6 3 3 GB

Students on 8 GB machines running the GNS3 VM (which itself wants 8 GB pinned — Appendix A4) will not fit A4 or A6. Have them stop the previous activity's nodes, and consider running A4 and A6 as demonstrations rather than individual builds if the room's hardware is thin. Note that A1 and A5, the two most important activities, cost nothing.

What actually goes wrong

  • A1: "Could not find Dynamips." The hub and switch are dynamips devices, and this stops the activity dead. It should have been fixed in week 1 §0a; check before the session.
  • A2: interfaces losing their addresses. Every OL9 activity from here to the end of term needs nmcli device set ethN managed no first. Students will forget. Put it on the board.
  • A2 part 3 is the payload of the whole week and it is one command (show arp on PC1). Do not let students skip it because it looks small. If they leave with one fact, this is the fact.
  • A4: firewalld. Pings across the router may be dropped. systemctl stop firewalld on the router. This will recur all term; consider disabling it in the image and telling students you did.
  • A5 Build 3 will lock up a slow machine. Warn first, and tell them how to stop the nodes from the toolbar before they build it. Nothing is damaged, but a student who cannot get control back panics.
  • A6's absolute speeds will be nonsense on fast hosts. Redirect attention to the ratio between rows. If tbf shapes unconvincingly, raise burst.

Things to say out loud

  • After A1's hub run: "Every machine on that hub read a conversation it was not part of. That was normal office networking until about 1998."
  • After A2: "Nothing on that box changed except which header it read. Hold on to that when someone tells you a router is a kind of computer — every one of these is a kind of computer."
  • After A4: "NAT is not protecting you. It is failing to know where to send things, and you are benefiting from its confusion."
  • After A5 Build 3: "You have just built the problem that the whole of next week's spanning tree exists to solve. Do not let anyone tell you STP is theoretical."

The gaps, and how to be honest about them

GNS3 models no physical layer at all. Say so explicitly rather than letting students infer that cables are fungible in reality.

Topic Handling
Straight-through vs crossover Packet Tracer refuses the wrong cable and is genuinely better here. A 10-minute PT demo covers it
Auto-MDI-X discussion only; it is why the previous row stopped mattering
Collisions / CSMA/CD week 3's unplugged activity. GNS3's hub does not collide
Wi-Fi cannot be done in GNS3 at any effort. Packet Tracer, or real hardware. Decide this term whether wireless is an outcome — if it is, you need PT alongside

Assessment

Still ungraded. The running project starts next week; use the last ten minutes of session 3 to hand out ../../project/README.md and ../../project/milestone1-switching/, so students arrive in week 3 having read the scenario.

Prep checklist

  • [ ] dynamips confirmed working on every machine (A1 blocker)
  • [ ] firewalld decision made — disabled in the image, or students told to stop it
  • [ ] RAM guidance for 8 GB machines, per the table above
  • [ ] Packet Tracer available if you are covering cabling or Wi-Fi
  • [ ] Project brief distributed at the end of session 3
