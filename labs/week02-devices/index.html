Week 2 — Devices and topologies

Assumes: week 1. Students can address a node, read a capture, and name the layer a header belongs to. OL9 is installed.

Leaves students able to: state what makes a device a hub, a switch or a router, and prove it by measurement rather than by picture; explain NAT's asymmetry; describe a topology by its failure modes.

The arc

Week 1 established that layers are real. This week asks: if the layers are real, what does a device do with each one? The answer is arranged as a ladder — a hub ignores every layer, a switch reads layer 2, a router reads layer 3, a NAT router rewrites it. Each rung is one activity and one measurement.

The centrepiece is A2, where the same node is turned from a switch into a router by configuration alone. Nothing sells "layer" like watching one box change which one it operates at.

# Activity Time Needs
A1 Hub vs switch ★ 35 min 4 VPCS + hub + switch + Wireshark
A2 One box, two devices ★ 50 min 1 OL9 (4 NICs) + 4 VPCS
A3 The NIC owns the address 25 min 2 OL9
A4 NAT from both sides 45 min 3 OL9
A5 Topology as failure modes 40 min 4 VPCS + hub + switch
A6 Sharing one wire 35 min 3 OL9

★ = keep these if the week compresses.

Sequencing note

A1 before A2. A1 establishes what a switch does by watching it from outside; A2 then builds one and takes it apart. Reversed, A2 is just a configuration exercise.

A6 carries packet-vs-circuit switching, which the original week-1 syllabus covered as vocabulary. It is here because this is the first week with a link worth sharing.

Deliverable

worksheet.md, sections 1–6. Section 2's device-comparison table is the one students should keep; week 4 fills in the routing column properly.

The two gaps, stated plainly

GNS3 has no physical layer. It does not model cable types, cable length, attenuation, or collisions. It will happily "connect" anything to anything.

Topic GNS3 What to do
Straight-through vs crossover absent Packet Tracer models it and refuses the wrong cable; or crimp real cable in the room
Auto-MDI-X absent discuss only — it is why the previous row stopped mattering after ~2000
Collisions, CSMA/CD absent week 3's unplugged activity, or Packet Tracer's animation
Wi-Fi absent entirely Packet Tracer, or real hardware. This is the one unclosable gap

Tell students the simulator's limits while they are using it. A student who believes GNS3 models physics will be badly wrong later, and the honesty costs nothing.
