Week 4 — instructor notes

Shape of the week

Session Do Note
1 A1, start A2 build A1 is short; getting w04-base cabled takes longer than expected
2 A2, A3 the two anchors; same project, do not delete between them
3 A4, A5 A5 pays off week 3's cliffhanger — do not drop it
4 A6, A7 same w04-base; A6 must precede A7
— A8, A9 + the reading extensions: homework, or drop

Compressed: A2, A3 and A6. A1 can be a ten-minute demonstration; A4, A5 and A7 can be homework; A8 and A9 are already optional.

A9 is the most droppable and the most rewarding. It is the only place students build ICMP rather than read it, and the forged-error section is the security payoff of A6. Treat it as an enrichment activity for students who finish A6/A7 early, or as a bridge into the network_sec/ module, which is its other natural home.

If the week is only three sessions, the honest trade is A5 or A6 — not both — and A6 is the one that changes how students diagnose everything afterwards. A5 can move to week 5 alongside subnetting; A6 cannot move without breaking A7.

RAM

w04-base is four OL9 nodes, ~3 GB — two Routers at 1 GB, two PCs at 512 MB — plus two Ethernet switches, which are free. A3 wants three simultaneous Wireshark windows on top. This is the term's peak so far.

If students build PC1 and PC2 from the Router template out of habit, the project costs 4 GB instead of 3. Worth checking before the session.

For 8 GB machines running the GNS3 VM (itself pinned at 8 GB — Appendix A4), this does not fit. Two workable options:

  • Pairs. One build per pair of students, both watching. A3 genuinely benefits from two people reading two captures.
  • Demonstration. Project A2 and A3 and have students fill in the same worksheet from your captures. They lose the build, not the observation.

A1 needs 2 nodes and A5 needs 2; both fit anywhere.

What actually goes wrong

  • firewalld blocks ICMP errors, which silently ruins A4 — no Time to live exceeded, and tracepath shows nothing but *. If you have not disabled it in the image, put systemctl stop firewalld on the board for the whole week.
  • The /30 transit trips people up. 10.0.0.0/30 has exactly two usable addresses. Students assign .3 or .0 and are confused. This is deliberate foreshadowing of week 5 — let it happen, then name it.
  • A2 part 1 only works if students resist adding both routes. Some will add the return route from habit and see success, destroying the activity. Have them run ip route show on R2 and read it aloud before pinging.
  • A2 part 4's loop route may be rejected by some kernels as invalid. The fallback is in the activity's troubleshooting table.
  • A3 requires all twelve addresses collected first. Students who start capturing before building the address table cannot interpret anything they see, conclude the captures are wrong, and rebuild the topology. Insist on the table.
  • A5: modprobe 8021q may be needed. Check on the image before the session.
  • firewalld now ruins three activities, not one. It swallows ICMP errors, so A4 shows nothing but *, A6's five cases collapse into five timeouts, and A7's path MTU discovery silently fails. If it is not disabled in the image, this belongs on the board for the whole week, not just A4's session.
  • A6 case 1 is the one students misread. They see "Network is unreachable" and assume a packet went out and something answered. Make them look at the empty capture. If they skip that, the rest of the activity has no anchor.
  • A6's reject needs the explicit form. A bare nft ... reject returns port unreachable, not admin prohibited, and the distinction is the point of the case. The activity spells it out; students copying from memory will not.
  • A6's redirect section modifies w04-base — one extra link and a repointed route on R1. The restore commands are at the end of the activity and A7 will fail confusingly without them. Check the class has run them before dismissing.
  • A7's black-hole demonstration needs the rule on R1's output chain, not forward. R1 generates those errors itself. Students who put it on forward see nothing happen and conclude the demonstration is broken.
  • A8 needs a NAT node and working egress from the GNS3 host. Test it yourself before the session — if the room's network blocks outbound ICMP, part 4 produces the right answer for the wrong reason, and the comparison is worthless. Have a screenshot ready as a fallback.
  • A8's source-routing section needs accept_source_route=1 on both routers. Setting it on one produces a confusing partial result.
  • A9's forged MTU may be ignored by a kernel with a high min_pmtu, or if the quoted inner packet is implausible. The activity's troubleshooting table covers both; the fix that usually works is lowering nexthopmtu toward 552 and matching a four-tuple the target would actually use.
  • A9 must run as root (sudo scapy) — raw sockets. Students who launch scapy unprivileged get a permission error on the first send.

Things to say out loud

  • Before A2 part 1: "Everything you can check from either end will pass. Notice how strong the urge is to start changing settings. That urge is what this activity is inoculating you against."
  • After A2: "Two devices, one conversation, two routes — and neither router is wrong on its own. Correctness here is a property of the pair, not of either configuration."
  • After A3: "Your IP address is who you are. Your MAC address is who is standing next to you."
  • After A4's loop: "Week 3's loop destroyed the network. This one killed one packet. One field."
  • After A5 part 3: "Those two frames were on the same wire, half a millisecond apart, in different broadcast domains — because both ends agreed to respect a number. That is all a VLAN is."
  • Before A6: "Every failure you have had this week said the same thing on screen. The network has been telling you far more than that, in every single case, and you have not been reading it."
  • After A6's redirect: "Nothing failed. The ping worked. And ICMP still had something to say — so whatever ICMP is, it is not the thing that shows up when you break something."
  • After A7's black hole: "Somebody blocked ICMP to be safe. What they actually did was remove the message that would have told them what was wrong."
  • After A8 part 4: "Nobody on that path is misconfigured. Every one of them made a reasonable decision. Between them they deleted a feature from the internet without changing a single standard."
  • After A9's forged error: "You are not either end of that connection, and you just changed it. The network believed a stranger because nothing in ICMP asks who is speaking. That is the whole reason the next section of the course exists."

Assessment

Milestone 2 opens this week (../../project/milestone2-static-routing/) and builds on the network graded in milestone 1.

Publish the milestone 1 reference topology at the same time. Any student whose milestone 1 was weak must be able to start milestone 2 from a known-good network, or one bad week compounds into a bad term. Marks for milestone 1 are already recorded; the reference build costs nothing and saves the students who most need saving. See ../../project/README.md.
